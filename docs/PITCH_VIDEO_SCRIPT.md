# Chloris pitch video script

**Target length:** about 7–8 minutes, safely below the 10-minute limit.
**Format:** voice-over with screen recording and short pauses for viewers to read the UI.
**Demo URL:** [live Chloris site](https://makeathon-2026-chloris-biodiversity.vercel.app/)

This script describes the build in the repository on 7 November 2026. Read the [README](../README.md) before recording. The score is `v0-draft`, sighting storage is device-only, and reliable PWA installation/offline caching are not yet implemented. Do **not** demonstrate cloud sync or airplane-mode browsing as completed features. Use the labelled cached scan example for a repeatable recording; if a live Pl@ntNet scan succeeds, it can be shown separately as live identification.

| Time | Picture / action | Spoken script |

| **0:00–0:45** Problem | Open on the live landing page. Let the Greece visual appear. Show the question on a simple title card. | “Where do Greece's medicinal-use plants grow, which might be at risk, and how can we explore that responsibly? The information exists, but it sits in plant checklists, biodiversity records and protected-area files. For someone walking outdoors or teaching about local flora, those files are difficult to use. We built Chloris to bring them together in one clear mobile web experience.” |
| **0:45–1:25** What Chloris does | Enter **Open Chloris**. Show Home, Plants, Map and a species page. | “Chloris brings a searchable plant catalogue, a map, species pages and photo identification into one bilingual interface. The current catalogue contains 1,386 plants native to Greece with a recorded medicinal-use flag. That phrase is precise: it is a documented use category, not a claim that the plant is an effective medicine or safe to collect.” |
| **1:25–2:25** Data and method | Show the [pipeline notebook](../pipeline/chloris_pipeline.ipynb) briefly, then a simple diagram from the [README](../README.md#how-it-works). Zoom into the map grid and a score breakdown. | “We start with Kew's World Checklist of Vascular Plants for accepted names and native ranges, then Kew's World Checklist of Useful Plant Species for the medicinal-use flag. We match those plants to GBIF taxonomy and observations, and combine the records with Natura 2000 protected areas. Our Python pipeline cleans and aggregates the observations into approximate ten-kilometre cells. A transparent draft score combines IUCN category, endemism, rarity, a recording-effort-corrected decline signal and the share of records outside protected areas. The fire component has no data in this export, so it is excluded. These weights still need science-lead review.” |

Each public square is roughly ten kilometres across. Tap a square to see its recorded species; change the layer to see different signals. The flowers sit on land even around islands, but their positions are representative points for a cell, never an individual plant's coordinates. The current export has 2,134 occupied cells.” |

The photo has a visible photographer and licence credit. A picture helps people recognise a plant, but it does not confirm an identification or make harvesting safe. We therefore show the warning before the detailed record.” |

## Recording checklist

- Record the live app and a short notebook/README view at **phone resolution**. Keep captions large enough to read and cut pauses rather than speeding up speech.
- Use the **Greek sage cached example** for the main scan demonstration. If you show a real upload, label it as live and make sure the Pl@ntNet key works before filming.
- Show an actual species photo **with its credit line**; do not crop away the licence. Avoid close-up maps of sensitive observations.
- If the team changes data or scoring before recording, re-read `web/public/data/meta.json` and update every narrated number. Confirm the science lead's wording before presenting the draft score as a conservation conclusion.
- Add the confirmed team names and roles to the closing card. Upload the finished video, then add its link to the Devpost page and README.
